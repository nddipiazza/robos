How a party runs. The host console needs at least one RobOS TV paired in Smart TV Studio and an AI agent picked with the shared <robos-agent-selector>. This is the one RobOS app where AI is required, because the AI host is the game. Guests scan the QR code on the TV and answer a survey: the basics, their interests, a few confessions and some wild cards. Until someone claims it, every phone shows I'm the party host. Whoever taps it describes the house: the usable spaces, what is special about them (guitar amps in the basement, a hot tub, a cat named Pancake), the games in the house, craft supplies, gear such as a printer, the vibe, and what is off-limits. The game cannot start until the required house answers are in. A meter on the phone encourages the party host to add more detail, because more detail makes better challenges.

The writers' room never makes the room wait. Agent CLIs take seconds to answer, so every AI call runs in the background ahead of when it is needed (lib/ai-host.js). A guest finishes the survey and gets a personal welcome on the TV. The party host finishes the house and the host gives a house tour. Once everyone is ready, three calls run in parallel: the show script (title, opening, role reasons, game intros, Who Said That? teasers, superlatives and bluffs), trivia about each guest's interests, and House Quests built from the house survey. Standings recaps are written during the last reveal of each mini game, and the finale awards during the last mini game. In a real run with Antigravity, each call took 16 to 33 seconds and all of them were ready before the host pressed Start. If a call is slow or fails, the host uses stock material, so no click ever waits on an agent.

Five mini games on one template. Who Said That?, Most Likely To, House Quest, Nerd Alert Trivia and Two Truths & a Bot are each a small file built on lib/minigames/template.js. A mini game builds its rounds from the guests, the house and whatever the AI wrote, scores the answers, and supplies the host's lines. The engine (lib/game-engine.js) handles the TVs, phones, timers, scoreboard and role perks. The Detective sees one wrong answer crossed out, the Wildcard can double down, the Trickster earns a point for every guest they fool, and the Mole has a secret mission that is revealed at the finale.

Getting a Roku to show and speak the game. Browser TVs (LG webOS, Samsung Tizen, Android TV) simply load the game's TV page. A Roku has no browser, so the shared TV engine gained app casts (robos-lib/tv-engine.js). The party game renders each Roku's screen in an offscreen window and pushes JPEG frames to the TV portal. The RobOS TV channel, rebuilt and sideloaded on cast just as a Dashboards cast does, shows those frames on its poster layer. Sound took two fixes, both confirmed on the TV through Roku's media-player query and debug console. A Roku has a single media pipeline, so the host's lines now play through the channel's keep-alive video player as small MP4 clips. That keep-alive stops the TV from going to standby, and its once-a-second check was restarting it over every line, so the check now steps aside while the host is talking: stop the keep-alive, play the line, start the keep-alive again. The same lines can also go to a RobOS Audio Player speaker. If music is playing on that speaker, the Audio Player's new control API plays the line on it and resumes the song where it left off.
