Until this week, the Starfall Command campaign was a menu page. You picked a mission from a list, chose a difficulty, and the battle started. The missions had grown into real stories, like the Hold for Dawn evacuation and the Last Road convoy, but nothing tied them together between battles.

Now the campaign lives on a ship. Between missions you stand on the flagship and move between four rooms: the Bridge, the Armory, the Cantina and the Laboratory. You click on people and objects in each room. Clicking the star map opens Mission Select. Clicking the quartermaster plays his briefing. The TV in the Cantina plays a news report about the war so far. A brand-new campaign starts with a short 3D film made from the game’s own models, and when the film ends Mission 01 begins. Every line spoken in the hub, the cutscenes and the mission films is voiced: 192 recordings, almost 20 minutes in total.

Four rooms instead of one menu

Each room is a painted backdrop with clickable hotspots placed over it. Hovering a hotspot draws a callout line to the person or object it belongs to. The Bridge has the Mission Console on the holographic star map, Captain Vale, the Mutator Matrix and a replay of the opening film. The Armory is a hangar bay. Clicking a vehicle on the floor, like the Rail Skimmer or the Warden Walker, opens its upgrades. The Cantina has Corporal Rook, the Frontier News TV, the jukebox, the mercenary contract board, a job board that also opens Mission Select, and the Cinema Archive. The Laboratory has Dr. Aster, a Brood specimen tank and a Concord relic.

The keyboard still works. M opens Mission Select, C opens the Cinema Archive, and Esc closes the open console first and returns to the title screen on the second press. The test suite checks that order.

What the consoles sell, and what that does today

A campaign starts with 100,000 credits, and each cleared mission pays between 45,000 and 135,000. The Armory’s Tech Purchase console lists 48 upgrades for 24 units in five categories: Base, Infantry, Vehicles, Starships and Special Ops. The Cantina’s contract board lists 6 mercenary companies. The first, the Bracken Roadwardens, is free, and the others cost 35,000 to 75,000 credits. The Laboratory has two research tracks, Gnarl Brood Xenobiology and Aurelian Concord Relics. Each has five tiers with a choice of two perks per tier, 20 perks in total. The tiers are paid for with research samples, not credits.

To be clear about the current state: purchases, contracts and perks are saved to the campaign save file, and credits are deducted correctly. The hub test buys the Rifleman’s Ballistic Riot Shield and checks that exactly 45,000 credits leave the balance. But the battle code doesn’t read any of these yet, so an upgrade you buy doesn’t change a unit in the next mission. Connecting the two is the next step for the hub.

Cutscenes that follow what you do

The new cutscene player starts scenes in response to what you do in the hub. The first time you enter the Armory, the Cantina or the Laboratory, that room plays its introduction once. Clicking Rook, Vale or the news TV plays their scenes. Buying an upgrade, signing a contract or finishing research plays a short scene in which the unit shows off its new gear. Clearing missions 01, 03, 08 and 11 queues the story scenes for Act I, Act II, Act III and the finale. Each one plays once, the next time you return to the flagship.

The Cinema Archive in the Cantina lets you rewatch scenes. It lists 11 story scenes and 11 mission movies. Act scenes stay locked until their mission is cleared, and a mission movie unlocks along with its mission.

Every one of the 11 missions now opens with a three-shot movie. The first shot is a 3D view of the fleet in orbit, or, for the first three missions, before the campaign has a flagship, a low flyover with Pilot Sable. The second is a slow push across the launch bay painting while the mission’s usual opening line plays. The third is a small 3D scene of that mission’s units in combat. The battle clock is held while the movie plays. When the movie ends, the gameplay camera starts at the mission’s landing point.

The opening film

A new campaign, with no missions cleared and the film never watched, now opens with a five-shot film called Operation: Cut the Signal. It is rendered in real time from the game’s own unit and building models. The skip button reads START MISSION 01 ▸, and when the film ends, by skipping or by finishing, Mission 01 launches directly. The mission would normally play its own pre-mission movie next. Here it is skipped, so you don’t sit through two intros in a row. The title screen also has an Intro Cinematic button to rewatch the film.

The first version, from earlier the same day, was a seven-shot montage of the whole war: an armor forge, a visor close-up, a fleet rollout, a warp jump, the Brood rising, Concord warships and a final clash. It looked like a trailer, but it didn’t lead anywhere. The remake tells one smaller story, how Vale’s squad ends up on the Vesper Gate road, and it ends at the exact moment the first mission begins.

How the film is put together

Each film is a list of shots described as data in RTSCutsceneData.gd. A shot has a kind, a voice cue, timed sound effects, a caption, a speaker and a portrait. RTSCutsceneTheater.gd builds the scene for each kind inside a SubViewport: ground plane, lights, fog, models and effects. A shot lasts for its scripted duration or for its voice line plus 0.45 seconds, whichever is longer, so a line never gets cut off. That makes the opening film about 45 seconds long.

The camera follows a quadratic Bézier curve. Each shot sets a start, a middle and an end point, and the theater blends them with de Casteljau’s method at an eased time u. At the same time, it moves the look-at point, changes the field of view, and adds a small handheld sway plus a decaying shake when shells land. Each shot in the opening film also plays sound effects at set times, such as a radar ping, an arc welder or rifle fire.

# RTSCutsceneTheater.gd, per frame var u := smoothstep(0.0, 1.0, progress) var q0 := _cam_from.lerp(_cam_mid, u) var q1 := _cam_mid.lerp(_cam_to, u) cam_pos = q0.lerp(q1, u) look_pt = _cam_look.lerp(_cam_look_to, u)

The handoff to the mission uses two flags. RTSCampaignHub.gd starts the opening sequence and, when the film finishes, sets a skip_redundant_m0_movie flag before launching Mission 01. RTSCampaignHud.gd reads and clears that flag, so it never suppresses a later replay.

192 voice lines from seven characters

generate_campaign_voices.py builds the script for every spoken line from the game data. That includes the mission contacts, all 48 Armory upgrades, the 6 contracts, the 20 perks and each hub hotspot. It then synthesizes the lines on the GPU with Qwen3-TTS. Four characters, Vale, Sable, Aster and Rook, are cloned with the 0.6B Base model from campaign recordings that already shipped with the game, so they sound the same in battle and on the ship. The other three, Quartermaster Vane, news anchor Kade Vance and the Starfall helm officer, had no recordings yet. For each one, the 1.7B VoiceDesign model generates a reference line from a written description, such as “older burly chief ordnance engineer… deep gravelly baritone”, and that reference is then cloned like the others.

Each clip is resampled to 22,050 Hz mono 16-bit, trimmed of silence, faded in and out to prevent clicks, and normalized to the same loudness. The script only synthesizes lines that don’t have a WAV yet, unless you pass --force. It then writes manifest.json with each line’s speaker, text and measured duration. The first pass produced 176 lines. The seven-shot film added 7 more, and the Vesper Gate remake added 9: five for the new film, three new low-altitude flyover lines for missions 01–03, and one early Cantina line. The manifest now has 192 lines and 1,188 seconds of speech. Vane has the most lines, 59, because he voices every Armory upgrade.

Testing and how the screenshots were taken

tests/test_campaign_hub.gd drives the hub through the same apply_ui_state calls that the control server exposes, using a throwaway save file. It checks that the room intros play once and skip cleanly, and that seven hub cutscenes start and stop when asked. It checks that each of the 11 missions has a three-shot movie with a real voice WAV on every shot. It also checks that every hub cutscene, Armory upgrade, contract, perk and hotspot has a WAV, along with the Esc order, a purchase, a contract, a perk and the Act I milestone. Both suites passed on the current tree:

$ godot --headless --path games/robos-rts --script res://tests/test_campaign_hub.gd Campaign Hub: passed (6.6 s wall time) $ godot --headless --path games/robos-rts --script res://tests/test_audio_sfx.gd PASS test_audio_sfx (121 SFX and 781 voice streams verified)

Every image in this post is a frame from the running game. A small script started the game in a virtual 1920×1080 display with a fresh campaign save. It drove the hub through the control server’s /ui endpoint, which accepts hub_room, hub_console and play_cutscene, and saved frames with /screenshot. That display has no GPU, so frames rendered slowly. The 45-second film took about three and a half minutes to play through, and the script kept taking frames until the film ended.

Run it yourself

# From games/robos-rts. Needs Godot 4.3 (GODOT_BIN or on PATH) ./play.sh # title screen; Campaign opens the flagship hub # (first run with no progress plays the opening film, then Mission 01) # Hub, cutscene and voice coverage tests godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/test_campaign_hub.gd godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/test_audio_sfx.gd # Regenerate any missing voice WAVs + manifest.json (CUDA GPU, qwen_tts package) python3 generate_campaign_voices.py python3 generate_campaign_voices.py --force # re-synthesize all 192
