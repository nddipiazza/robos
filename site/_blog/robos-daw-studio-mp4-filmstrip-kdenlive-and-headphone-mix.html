Nothing sharpens a workstation faster than cutting real drum and guitar sessions in a loud room. Right after shipping 20-channel Linux drum tracking and HD Video Mode + MIDI Karaoke, we mounted an Android phone running RobOS IP Camera directly on the studio ceiling above our 12-mic acoustic kit, tracked multi-take covers (including Fall Out Boy — "XO"), and took those sessions all the way to finished vertical split-screen videos. Every friction point we hit during live tracking and post-production turned directly into the six upgrades below.

1. Real Overhead Studio Rig + Clean, Auto-Fullscreen RobOS IP Camera Capture

When an Android phone doubles as both a 24/7 home surveillance camera for RobOS Camera Watch and an overhead 1080p studio drum camera for RobOS DAW Studio, two camera behaviors immediately matter: you never want a surveillance timestamp burned into your music video takes, and when the phone is clamped horizontally overhead, you want a clean edge-to-edge viewfinder rather than a cramped portrait settings screen.

  • OSD-Free Clean DAW Camera Stream (?timestamp=0): During DAW recording, VideoTrackRecorder (packages/daw-studio/lib/video-devices.js) appends ?timestamp=0 to the camera stream URL and posts {"overlayTimestamp": false} to /api/control, restoring the previous setting when recording stops. On Android (HttpOnvifServer.kt, Camera2Engine.kt), FrameHub.cleanViewers suppresses the burned-in timestamp OSD across active DAW capture sessions with an instant EOF disconnect watcher so Camera Watch surveillance resumes normal timestamps the moment your session finishes.
  • Automatic Edge-to-Edge Landscape Fullscreen Viewfinder: Rotating the phone into landscape while the camera service is running (MainActivity.kt, Screens.kt) now unlocks sensor orientation, hides Android status/navigation bars via WindowInsetsControllerCompat, and automatically fills the entire 16:9 screen with the live camera output so you can check drum framing from the throne at a glance (with a 1-tap Fullscreen button in portrait and double-tap / floating pill to return to controls).

2. NLE Video Filmstrip Thumbnails Inside Timeline Video Tracks

Previously, video-only camera clips rendered as solid violet blocks on the arrangement timeline—making it hard to spot visually when the drummer sat down at the kit, picked up sticks, or finished a pass without scrubbing blindly. Video lanes now render true non-linear editor (NLE) keyframe filmstrips directly inside every video clip (generateVideoFilmstripSprite, drawVideoFilmstripIntoClip):

  • Cached 40-Frame FFmpeg Sprite Strips + 2-Worker Limiter: Each recorded or imported .mp4/.mkv/.webm clip generates a single horizontal JPEG sprite sheet (up to 40 evenly spaced 160x90 tiles + JSON metadata sidecar) keyed by file path, size, and mtime under ~/.cache/robos/daw-studio/filmstrips/. A 2-worker background concurrency limiter keeps UI scrolling at 60 fps even on multi-camera projects and reloads cached filmstrips instantaneously across restarts.
  • Zoom- & Trim-Accurate Tiling + Cinema Sprocket Rails: As you zoom in/out or slip-trim left/right clip handles, each visual tile samples the exact sprite frame corresponding to clip.offsetSec + localSec, framed by subtle cinema film-sprocket perforations along the top and bottom edges.
  • Live Recording Snapshots & Hybrid Audio+Video Backdrop: While recording a live camera take, DAW Studio snapshots the live monitor feed every ~1.5 s so filmstrip tiles grow across the growing red recording region in real time before seamlessly swapping to the final FFmpeg sprite strip on stop. Hybrid clips that carry both video and paired audio render a dimmed filmstrip backdrop behind the high-contrast transient waveform.

3. 1-Click MP4 Mixdown, Idempotent Kdenlive (.kdenlive) Sync & Bottom-Bar Background Jobs

Once you nail a drum or guitar take, you either want an instant shareable .mp4 combining your camera video with your mixed 17-track studio audio, or you want to jump straight into Kdenlive for multi-camera split-screens without manually aligning WAVs and MP4s by hand (mixdown-exporter.js, kdenlive-exporter.js):

  • 1-Click MP4 Video + Stereo Console Mixdown: Accessible from the Export MP4 button in the Video Output header, right-clicking any video track/clip, or Ctrl+Shift+E. Renders your unmuted DAW console tracks (parametric EQ, VCA compression, drum triggers, and Master Drum Glue at -0.3 dBFS) and muxes them onto your chosen camera take (defaulting automatically to the latest recorded take). Supports Instant Lossless Video Copy (-c:v copy + 320 kbps AAC) when clip start aligns, plus 1080p CRF 18 and 720p CRF 20 H.264 re-encodes with black-frame tpad timeline lead-in alignment and From -> To range trimming.
  • Idempotent Save & Re-Sync to Kdenlive (.kdenlive): Clicking Kdenlive renders a 24-bit WAV studio mixdown alongside an MLT XML .kdenlive project containing [RobOS] Audio Mixdown and frame-accurate [RobOS] Video — <Camera> tracks (with raw camera scratch audio muted automatically so only the polished DAW console mix plays). Best of all, export is idempotent: tweaking your kick EQ in RobOS DAW Studio and re-saving into the same .kdenlive file refreshes the [RobOS] audio/video producers while preserving every custom cut, title card, color grade, and split-screen track you added inside Kdenlive.
  • Non-Blocking <robos-job-status> Bottom-Toolbar Queue: Exports no longer freeze your session behind a blocking modal. Clicking Export asks for the destination path first (daw:pick-export-path), immediately closes the dialog so you can keep playing or tweaking mixes, and streams live progress in the reusable <robos-job-status> Web Component embedded in the 24px bottom statusbar—unifying VIDEO MP4, MIXDOWN, KDENLIVE, CLICK AI, MIDI .KAR, and STEMS jobs with live percentage, expandable popover history, and a 1-click Reveal button.

4. Scarlett 18i20 Hardware DSP Headphone Mix, True Beat-1 Click AI & Queued Ctrl+Z

Tracking acoustic drums in headphones surfaced three classic studio problems: Focusrite's internal hardware direct-monitor matrix was feeding raw drum mic bleed straight into our headphones and drowning out the backing song; 3/4 vs. 6/8 songs needed meter-aware downbeat accents; and hitting Ctrl+Z the instant a bad take stopped could race file finalization.

  • Hardware DSP Direct-Monitor vs. Backing Balance: The new topbar Phones Mix button programs the Scarlett 18i20 / Clarett+ internal hardware mixer over mainline ALSA amixer (packages/robos-lib/audio-devices.js)—attenuating or muting live hardware mic inputs routed to headphone Mix A/Mix B (-80 dB..+12 dB across matrix controls) without touching pre-fader recording levels, while boosting USB PCM 1+2 playback, PipeWire sink volume, and up to +6 dB WebAudio backing trim.
  • Time-Signature-First Click Track AI (True Beat-1 Lock): click-track-generator.js now evaluates meter candidates (4/4, 3/4, 6/8, 5/4, 7/8) before synthesizing clicks—using a dedicated 3/4 vs. 6/8 compound-duple hemiola test and asymmetric low-band bass/kick onset energy so the high downbeat woodblock lands on the real musical Beat 1 rather than Beat 3.
  • PipeWire Cork-Safe Latency Guard & Queued Ctrl+Z: computeReliableEarsStartWallMs cross-checks AudioContext.getOutputTimestamp() against wall-clock startup to reject stale PipeWire cork epochs, and pressing Ctrl+Z while FFmpeg/ALSA is still closing a recording pass queues cleanly behind activeRecordStopPromise so rapid punch-in retakes never race or orphan files.
