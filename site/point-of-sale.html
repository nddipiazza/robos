RobOS Point of Sale · Retail, food service and bars

An open-source cash register that runs on any touchscreen

Take orders, scan items, split checks, run self-checkout, track stock and count the drawer, with no subscription and no cloud account. Your data stays in one file on your own machine. This guide is built from the project's own tests: 50 checked against the real server and 21 tapped through in a touch-enabled browser, across a clothing store, a coffee shop and a bar. Screenshots come from those runs, using labelled test data.

Sample 1 · Retail

Clothing store

Threadwell Clothing sells tees, jeans and jackets in sizes, each with its own barcode and stock count. Scan a tag or tap an item and pick a size. Promo codes, returns and exchanges are built in, and a shopper can scan their own items at the self-checkout.

Use cases checked against the real server (19)
  1. cashier opens the drawer, scans barcodes and takes a cash sale with change
  2. scanning the same barcode twice stacks quantity; unknown barcodes are reported
  3. a size must be chosen for clothing with variants
  4. card sale: a declined card leaves the order open and stock untouched, then a good card succeeds
  5. promo codes: percent code, minimum spend for a fixed code, no stacking the same code
  6. manual discounts above the cashier limit need a manager PIN, and a reason
  7. stock control: cannot oversell, receiving stock lets the sale go through
  8. returns: cashier needs a manager, partial refund restocks and reduces the drawer; cannot over-refund
  9. exchange: refund the small, sell the large, stock follows
  10. split tender: part cash, part card
  11. cash rules: must open the drawer first, tender must cover the balance, absurd tenders rejected
  12. inventory input: add a product with sizes and opening stock, validate barcodes, reject duplicates
  13. inventory input: receive, count-adjust with reason, CSV import, low-stock list, export
  14. end of day: count the drawer by denomination, small variance ok, large variance needs a manager
  15. cash payment administration: manager turns cash off, sets rounding and quick-tender buttons
  16. self-checkout: customer builds a basket, pays by card; or chooses pay-at-counter and the cashier takes cash
  17. open checkout API: key required, create, update, complete (idempotent), decline, cancel
  18. receipt is also available as schema.org JSON-LD
  19. sales report and CSV export reflect the day
Use cases tapped through on a touchscreen browser (7)
  1. cashier scans a barcode, taps a size for jeans, applies a promo code and takes a card; then handles a return with manager approval
  2. cash sale at the register: change due is shown, receipt prints, drawer is required first
  3. manager does inventory input: new stock item, receive a delivery, count the shelf, and import a CSV
  4. owner adds a clothing item with sizes and a valid barcode; a bad barcode is refused with a clear message
  5. self-checkout in the store: shopper scans a tag, picks a size by touch, chooses pay-at-counter; cashier finds the order and takes cash
  6. shopper at self-checkout who backs out leaves nothing behind for the staff
  7. reports: manager sees the day by category and tender, and a cashier cannot open reports

Sample 2 · Food service

Coffee shop

Bean There Coffee sells drinks and food. A latte needs a size and a milk; extras cost extra. Every cup uses up beans, milk and a cup from stock. Paid orders appear on the barista and kitchen screens, and a customer-facing board shows the number when it is ready. Customers can order and pay themselves at the self-checkout.

Use cases checked against the real server (18)
  1. modifiers: required choices are enforced and priced; options from other items are refused
  2. the same drink with the same choices stacks; different choices stay separate lines
  3. order types: dine-in and take-out are allowed, anything else is not
  4. recipes: a latte uses cups, espresso beans and the chosen milk; extra shots use more beans
  5. running out of an ingredient blocks only the drinks that need it
  6. prep queue: paid drinks go to the barista screen, food to the kitchen, and the ready board shows the number
  7. tips: percent presets and fixed amounts flow into the total, card payment and reports
  8. cash with nickel rounding: the total rounds, change is exact, and a Z-report balances
  9. worker corrections: unsent items vanish, sent items need a manager and a reason, whole orders can be voided
  10. two taps on Pay cannot charge twice
  11. menu input: manager-level admin adds categories, modifier groups and items; edits prices; hides items
  12. team: add workers with PINs, PINs are unique, wrong PINs lock the screen briefly, deactivating signs them out
  13. self-checkout at the coffee shop: order, tip, pay by card, appears on the barista screen
  14. self-checkout can be switched off or restricted by the owner; sold-out items are flagged
  15. first-run setup: an empty shop offers samples once; afterwards only an admin may replace data
  16. data survives a restart (written atomically to disk)
  17. local server hardening: JSON only (no cross-site form posts), loopback host names only, safe headers
  18. abandoned self-checkout baskets are voided after half an hour and never clutter the staff tab list
Use cases tapped through on a touchscreen browser (7)
  1. worker rings a custom latte, adds a tip, takes cash with quick-tender, then it flows to prep and the ready board
  2. self-checkout: customer picks a drink and a muffin, tips and pays by card, and the barista sees it
  3. self-checkout: customer who prefers cash takes a number, and the cashier collects at the counter
  4. owner adds a new menu item from the Menu & Catalog screen and a worker can sell it immediately
  5. manager turns on nickel rounding in Cash settings and the register honours it
  6. end of shift: cashier counts the drawer with bill and coin buttons; an exact count closes with zero variance
  7. first run: an empty shop offers sample businesses, then the team signs in with PINs; wrong PIN is refused

Sample 3 · Bar

Bar

The Rusty Anchor runs on open tabs. Rounds are sent to the bar and the kitchen as they are ordered, happy-hour prices apply by themselves, and alcohol cannot be paid for until someone has checked ID. Comps and voids need a manager and a reason. Checks can be split by person or by item.

Use cases checked against the real server (13)
  1. open a tab, order rounds, send each round to the bar, then close the tab with a card and tip
  2. a snacks-only tab does not need an ID check
  3. happy hour: weekday 4-6pm is 25% off drinks, not on food, not at 7pm, not on Saturday
  4. pour sizes and recipes: pitchers, doubles and fractional wine bottles deplete the right stock
  5. an empty keg blocks pitchers but a short pour of something else still works
  6. comps and voids are manager actions with a reason, and show on the receipt
  7. split the check evenly three ways: card, cash with change, card; the pennies always add up
  8. split by items: each guest pays for their own drinks, and a settled item cannot be removed or paid twice
  9. cash tips and tip-out: tip on a cash sale, then a manager pays the bartenders out of the drawer
  10. two bartenders run separate tabs at once; both show on the open-tabs list
  11. alcohol is never sold unattended: self-checkout hides it and rejects it; the open API rejects it
  12. refunding a bar tab is a manager action; drinks already poured are not put back in the keg
  13. close of night: Z-report shows sales by category, tenders, tips and the drawer count
Use cases tapped through on a touchscreen browser (7)
  1. bartender opens a tab, sends rounds, holds it, recalls it, checks ID, adds a tip and closes with a card
  2. happy hour: the 4-6pm price shows on the ticket automatically
  3. comp a drink: needs a manager PIN and a reason; the ticket drops to zero and the comp is on the receipt
  4. split the check two ways: first half by card, second half in cash with change
  5. pay by item: each guest settles their own items, one after the other
  6. the bar does not offer unattended self-checkout
  7. works on a small phone-sized screen too (responsive layout)

Every business

The cash drawer and cash settings

Cash is handled the way a till is: a counted float at the start, paid-in and paid-out entries with a reason, a blind count by bill and coin at the end, and a Z-report that shows over or short. Managers decide whether cash is accepted at all, whether prices round to the nearest 5 cents, what the quick-tender buttons are, and how much over/short a cashier may close without a manager. The cash scenarios appear under each business above.

For developers

The open checkout API

Websites, apps and agents can create a checkout session, update it, and complete it with a payment token. The shape follows the open Agentic Commerce Protocol checkout flow; amounts are whole cents and retries are safe with an Idempotency-Key. Card payments go through a plug-in provider, so the register never touches card numbers. The OpenAPI document is served at /api/openapi.json.

curl -X POST http://127.0.0.1:3410/api/v1/checkout_sessions \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $POS_KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"items":[{"id":"<item id, SKU or barcode>","quantity":2}],"fulfillment_option_id":"takeout"}'

curl -X POST http://127.0.0.1:3410/api/v1/checkout_sessions/<id>/complete \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $POS_KEY" -H "Idempotency-Key: order-1001" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"payment_data":{"token":"tok_visa","provider":"mock"}}'
