RobOS Point of Sale · Retail, food service and bars

An open-source cash register that runs on any touchscreen

Take orders, scan items, split checks, run self-checkout, track stock and count the drawer, with no subscription and no cloud account. Your data stays in one file on your own machine. This guide is built from the project's own tests: 54 checked against the real server and 34 tapped through in a touch-enabled browser, across a general retail store, a coffee shop and a bar. Scan with a barcode gun or a webcam, from classic UPCs to QR codes. Screenshots come from those runs, using labelled test data.

Step 1

Install it and open your first shop

It is a normal RobOS app. You can also run it on any computer with Node.js, with no account and no internet connection.

  1. Open it. Launch RobOS Point of Sale from the App Launcher, or run cd packages/point-of-sale && npm install && npm start.
  2. Pick a starting point. On first launch choose Retail store, Coffee shop or Bar to get a ready-made menu, stock, taxes and team. You can edit everything afterwards, or replace it with another sample from Settings.
  3. Sign in with a PIN. The samples use owner 1111, manager 2222 and cashier 3333. Add your own team in Team & API and retire the sample PINs before real use.
  4. Open the drawer. Go to Cash, count your starting cash, type it on the number pad and press Open drawer. Card sales do not need the drawer; cash sales do.
  5. Ring a sale. On the Register tap items (or scan them with a scanner or the camera), press Pay, choose cash or card. The receipt appears with any change due.

Your data lives in one file, ~/.config/robos/point-of-sale/store.json, readable only by you. Back it up by copying it.

Step 2

Who can do what

Every team member signs in with a 4 to 8 digit PIN. Lower roles can borrow a manager for a single action without switching users.

RoleEveryday workAlso allowed
Cashier / serverSell, hold tabs, take cash and card, discounts up to the limit, prep screen, open and close their own drawer—
ManagerEverything a cashier doesRefunds, comps, voiding sent items, big discounts, paid in / paid out, receiving and counting stock, reports, cash payment settings, closing a drawer that is over or short
AdminEverything a manager doesMenu and catalog, taxes, promo codes, happy-hour rules, team, API keys, settings, loading samples

When a cashier taps something that needs a manager (a refund, a comp, a 40% discount), a PIN pad appears. The manager types their PIN, the action goes through, and the approval is recorded in the audit log next to the cashier's name. Five wrong PINs in a row lock the sign-in screen for a minute.

Step 3

The screens

Each screen is built for fingers: large buttons, an on-screen number pad for PINs, cash and quantities, and no tiny controls. Text fields such as item names use the device's own on-screen keyboard.

ScreenWhat you do there
RegisterChoose the order type (dine in, take-out, tab, retail), tap items, pick sizes and options, apply promo codes or discounts, add a tip, check ID, send to the kitchen or bar, hold, void or pay. Pay supports exact change, quick-tender buttons, split by 2, 3 or 4, or by item.
TabsOpen tabs and self-checkout orders waiting to be paid. Tap one to carry on. Type an order number to find a customer's order.
PrepOne screen per station (kitchen, barista, bar). Start, Ready, Picked up. The customer-facing board at /ready shows numbers that are ready.
OrdersRecent orders and receipts. Open one to refund items (a manager approves) and put returned items back in stock.
InventoryReceive a delivery, count the shelf (a reason is required), add stock items, import a CSV, spot low stock. Food and drink recipes use ingredients up automatically.
Menu & CatalogItems with sizes, barcodes and prep stations; option groups such as Size and Milk; categories; taxes; promo codes; happy-hour rules. Hiding an item keeps its history.
CashOpen and close the drawer, paid in and paid out, and the cash payment settings.
ReportsSales by category, tender and item for today or all time, drawer history, CSV export.
Team & APIAdd or deactivate team members and create checkout API keys.
SettingsBusiness name and type, tax included in prices or added on top, tipping, card provider, self-checkout options, stock rules, and sample businesses.

Step 4

Setting up cash payments

Managers and admins decide how cash behaves. Open Cash and scroll to Cash payment settings.

SettingWhat it does
Accept cash paymentsTurn off for a card-only shop. The Cash button disappears from the pay sheet and the server refuses cash.
Require an open drawerOn by default. Cash sales and cash refunds need a counted drawer so the day always balances.
Cash roundingNone, or nearest 5, 10, 25 cents or $1 for places without pennies. The rounding shows as its own line on the receipt and in the Z-report.
Quick-tender buttonsUp to six dollar amounts shown on the pay sheet, plus Exact and the next whole dollar.
Largest cash tenderGuards against a typo like $5000 instead of $50.
Over/short limitHow far off a cashier's count may be before a manager has to approve closing the drawer.
Bills and coins countedThe denominations shown as tap-to-count buttons when closing. The count is blind: cashiers do not see the expected total until they have counted.

End of shift: Cash → Count & close drawer, tap the bills and coins, press Close drawer. The Z-report shows expected, counted and over or short, plus the day's sales.

Step 5

Self-checkout and customer displays

Self-checkout is a customer-facing page that only does one thing: build a basket and pay. It cannot see staff data, prices of hidden items, or other customers' orders.

  • Open it from RobOS Point of Sale → Open self-checkout window, or browse to /kiosk. For a dedicated screen, start the app with --kiosk.
  • A tablet on the shop network. Run node serve.js --host 0.0.0.0 on the till computer and open http://<till-ip>:3410/kiosk on the tablet. There is no encryption on this connection, so use a network you trust.
  • Retail: a scan box accepts a barcode scanner that types into a field, and a camera button reads barcodes and QR codes from a webcam. Food: big icon tiles, required choices, tips.
  • Paying. Card is shown only when the card provider can run unattended (a gateway, not a standalone terminal where a worker types the approval code). Pay at the counter gives the customer a number; the cashier finds it under Tabs and takes cash.
  • Safety. Age-restricted items are never offered, and the server rejects them even if someone calls the API directly. Baskets nobody paid for are cleared after 30 minutes. Switch self-checkout off, or turn off pay-at-the-counter, in Settings.
  • Order-ready board. Put /ready on a second screen; it shows pick-up numbers as the kitchen marks them ready.

Sample 1 · Retail

Retail store

Main Street General Store stands in for any shop that sells things on shelves: apparel in sizes, home goods, electronics, groceries and stationery. Sized goods carry a barcode per size; plain goods have one barcode; groceries can be tax-exempt while everything else is taxed. Scan a tag or tap an item, apply promo codes, take returns and exchanges, and let shoppers scan their own items at the self-checkout. The same screens work for a hardware store, a bookshop, a pharmacy counter or a market stall.

  1. cashier opens the drawer, scans barcodes and takes a cash sale with change
  2. scanning the same barcode twice stacks quantity; unknown barcodes are reported
  3. a size must be chosen for sized goods (variants)
  4. card sale: a declined card leaves the order open and stock untouched, then a good card succeeds
  5. promo codes: percent code, minimum spend for a fixed code, no stacking the same code
  6. manual discounts above the cashier limit need a manager PIN, and a reason
  7. stock control: cannot oversell, receiving stock lets the sale go through
  8. returns: cashier needs a manager, partial refund restocks and reduces the drawer; cannot over-refund
  9. exchange: refund the small, sell the large, stock follows
  10. split tender: part cash, part card
  11. cash rules: must open the drawer first, tender must cover the balance, absurd tenders rejected
  12. inventory input: add a product with sizes and opening stock, validate barcodes, reject duplicates
  13. inventory input: receive, count-adjust with reason, CSV import, low-stock list, export
  14. end of day: count the drawer by denomination, small variance ok, large variance needs a manager
  15. cash payment administration: manager turns cash off, sets rounding and quick-tender buttons
  16. self-checkout: customer builds a basket, pays by card; or chooses pay-at-counter and the cashier takes cash
  17. open checkout API: key required, create, update, complete (idempotent), decline, cancel
  18. receipt is also available as schema.org JSON-LD
  19. sales report and CSV export reflect the day
  20. scanning understands every way a code can arrive: UPC-A, EAN-13, GTIN-14, UPC-E, GS1 Digital Link, element string
  21. barcode entry: a Digital Link typed or scanned into the item form is saved as the plain GTIN, and duplicates are caught across forms
  22. self-checkout lookup: finds menu items by any code, hides age-restricted and sold-out items, asks for a size when a code names only the product
  23. the retail sample is a general store: several departments, mixed tax, sized and unsized goods

Coffee shop

Bean There Coffee sells drinks and food. A latte needs a size and a milk; extras cost extra. Every cup uses up beans, milk and a cup from stock. Paid orders appear on the barista and kitchen screens, and a customer-facing board shows the number when it is ready. Customers can order and pay themselves at the self-checkout.

  2. the same drink with the same choices stacks; different choices stay separate lines
  3. order types: dine-in and take-out are allowed, anything else is not
  4. recipes: a latte uses cups, espresso beans and the chosen milk; extra shots use more beans
  5. running out of an ingredient blocks only the drinks that need it
  6. prep queue: paid drinks go to the barista screen, food to the kitchen, and the ready board shows the number
  7. tips: percent presets and fixed amounts flow into the total, card payment and reports
  8. cash with nickel rounding: the total rounds, change is exact, and a Z-report balances
  9. worker corrections: unsent items vanish, sent items need a manager and a reason, whole orders can be voided
  10. two taps on Pay cannot charge twice
  11. menu input: manager-level admin adds categories, modifier groups and items; edits prices; hides items
  12. team: add workers with PINs, PINs are unique, wrong PINs lock the screen briefly, deactivating signs them out
  13. self-checkout at the coffee shop: order, tip, pay by card, appears on the barista screen
  14. self-checkout can be switched off or restricted by the owner; sold-out items are flagged
  15. first-run setup: an empty shop offers samples once; afterwards only an admin may replace data
  16. data survives a restart (written atomically to disk)
  17. local server hardening: JSON only (no cross-site form posts), loopback host names only, safe headers
  18. abandoned self-checkout baskets are voided after half an hour and never clutter the staff tab list

Sample 3 · Bar

Bar

The Rusty Anchor runs on open tabs. Rounds are sent to the bar and the kitchen as they are ordered, happy-hour prices apply by themselves, and alcohol cannot be paid for until someone has checked ID. Comps and voids need a manager and a reason. Checks can be split by person or by item.

Use cases checked against the real server (13)
  1. open a tab, order rounds, send each round to the bar, then close the tab with a card and tip
  2. a snacks-only tab does not need an ID check
  3. happy hour: weekday 4-6pm is 25% off drinks, not on food, not at 7pm, not on Saturday
  4. pour sizes and recipes: pitchers, doubles and fractional wine bottles deplete the right stock
  5. an empty keg blocks pitchers but a short pour of something else still works
  6. comps and voids are manager actions with a reason, and show on the receipt
  7. split the check evenly three ways: card, cash with change, card; the pennies always add up
  8. split by items: each guest pays for their own drinks, and a settled item cannot be removed or paid twice
  9. cash tips and tip-out: tip on a cash sale, then a manager pays the bartenders out of the drawer
  10. two bartenders run separate tabs at once; both show on the open-tabs list
  11. alcohol is never sold unattended: self-checkout hides it and rejects it; the open API rejects it
  12. refunding a bar tab is a manager action; drinks already poured are not put back in the keg
  13. close of night: Z-report shows sales by category, tenders, tips and the drawer count
Use cases tapped through on a touchscreen browser (7)
  1. bartender opens a tab, sends rounds, holds it, recalls it, checks ID, adds a tip and closes with a card
  2. happy hour: the 4-6pm price shows on the ticket automatically
  3. comp a drink: needs a manager PIN and a reason; the ticket drops to zero and the comp is on the receipt
  4. split the check two ways: first half by card, second half in cash with change

Retail and anywhere with a shelf

Barcodes, QR codes and the webcam

Plug in any USB barcode scanner (it types into the scan box) or just use a webcam. The camera reads the classic UPC and EAN barcodes and the newer 2D codes: QR codes, Data Matrix and Aztec, including GS1 Digital Link QR codes that carry a product's GTIN as a web address. The register works out which product a code means, however it was printed: UPC-A, EAN-13, GTIN-14, the compressed UPC-E, a Digital Link, or a GS1 element string with a lot number. The same camera button fills in the barcode when you add a new product, and shoppers can use it at self-checkout.

Use cases tapped through on a touchscreen browser (13)
  1. register camera reads a UPC-A barcode and adds the item once, even if it stays in view
  2. the same product printed as an EAN-13 (UPC-A with a leading zero) is recognised
  3. a modern QR code carrying a GS1 Digital Link is read and matched by its GTIN
  4. a GS1 Data Matrix (GTIN plus lot number) is read
  5. an Aztec code holding a SKU is read
  6. a QR code holding a plain SKU is read
  7. a sized product is added with the exact size its barcode names
  8. an EAN-8 barcode (small packages) works once the shop has an item with that code
  9. a valid code that is not in the catalog is explained, and nothing is added
  10. self-checkout: a shopper holds a QR tag to the camera and it lands in the basket
  11. self-checkout: an unknown or age-restricted code is refused with a friendly message
  12. item form: scanning a QR with a GS1 Digital Link fills the barcode field, and it is saved as a plain GTIN
  13. without a camera, or with access blocked, the scanner says what is wrong

Every business

The cash drawer and cash settings

Cash is handled the way a till is: a counted float at the start, paid-in and paid-out entries with a reason, a blind count by bill and coin at the end, and a Z-report that shows over or short. Managers decide whether cash is accepted at all, whether prices round to the nearest 5 cents, what the quick-tender buttons are, and how much over/short a cashier may close without a manager. The cash scenarios appear under each business above.

For developers

The open checkout API

Websites, apps and agents can create a checkout session, update it, and complete it with a payment token. The shape follows the open Agentic Commerce Protocol checkout flow; amounts are whole cents and retries are safe with an Idempotency-Key. Card payments go through a plug-in provider, so the register never touches card numbers. The OpenAPI document is served at /api/openapi.json.

Endpoints

Method and pathPurpose
POST /api/v1/checkout_sessionsCreate a session from items (id, SKU or barcode, quantity, optional variant_id and modifiers), optional buyer and fulfillment_option_id. Returns 201.
GET /api/v1/checkout_sessions/{id}Current status, line items and totals.
POST /api/v1/checkout_sessions/{id}Change items, buyer, fulfillment option or add a promo code.
POST /api/v1/checkout_sessions/{id}/completePay with payment_data.token (and provider). A declined card returns 402 and the session stays open.
POST /api/v1/checkout_sessions/{id}/cancelCancel an unpaid session.

Status moves not_ready_for_payment → ready_for_payment → completed (or canceled). Totals come back as a list of items_base_amount, subtotal, tax and total in cents. Age-restricted items are refused at completion. Send Authorization: Bearer <key>; create keys in Team & API. This follows the shape of the Agentic Commerce Protocol checkout flow but is not a certified implementation, so test an agent against it before relying on it.

curl -X POST http://127.0.0.1:3410/api/v1/checkout_sessions \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $POS_KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"items":[{"id":"<item id, SKU or barcode>","quantity":2}],"fulfillment_option_id":"takeout"}'

curl -X POST http://127.0.0.1:3410/api/v1/checkout_sessions/<id>/complete \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $POS_KEY" -H "Idempotency-Key: order-1001" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"payment_data":{"token":"tok_visa","provider":"mock"}}'

Good to know

What it does not do yet

Being honest about the edges saves surprises on opening day.

  • Card processing. The built-in card options are a standalone terminal (you key in its approval code) and a demo gateway that approves any card, which must never be used for real sales. To take cards online or unattended, add a provider for your processor; the README shows the small interface.
  • Tax. A flat rate per item, either added on top or already included in prices. There is no address-based lookup, so confirm your local rules.
  • Hardware. Barcode scanners that type into a text box work, and so does a webcam. The browser only allows camera use on localhost or an https:// address, so a tablet that opens the till over plain http:// on the shop network cannot use its camera; give it a hardware scanner or put the till behind HTTPS. Receipts print through the system print dialog and are also available as plain text for ESC/POS printers. There are no direct cash-drawer or printer drivers yet.
  • Not included yet: gift cards, loyalty, several locations syncing together, staff time clocks.
  • Backups. One shop file on one computer. Copy it regularly.
