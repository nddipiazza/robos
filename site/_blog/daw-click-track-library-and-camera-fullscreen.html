What a click track is, and why this matters

A click track is a metronome recorded as its own track in your session: a steady tick on every beat that only the players hear in their headphones. Drummers use it to stay locked to the song when they play along to a backing track, and because every take then sits on the same grid, takes can be cut together, layered and lined up with video later.

The catch is that the click has to match the song. Real recordings speed up and slow down a little, songs can be in 3/4 or 6/8 instead of 4/4, and software that only listens to the audio can easily pick half or double the real tempo, or start the bar on the wrong beat. In the previous DAW Studio post we made the automatic click generator work out the time signature first. This time we let the drummer help: you tap along to the song, and DAW Studio uses your taps together with the song's own drum hits. You know where beat 1 is; the audio knows the exact timing. Together they get both right.

What's new, in short:

  • A Create Click Track window with three steps: pick your input and sounds, tap along after a countdown, then let Gemini check the tempo map and build the click.
  • A click track library of 16 metronome sounds (hi-hats, ride, sticks, cowbells, woodblocks, tambourine, shaker, a classic electronic click, snare and kick) and 8 ready-made pairs. One sound plays on ordinary beats and a second marks the "transition" beat, so you can hear where the bar turns around.
  • An Auto-Generate (No Tap) button for when you just want a click from the song audio and Gemini, without tapping.
  • A fullscreen icon and double-tap on the camera viewers we use to frame the drum kit (covered at the end).

Using it

  1. Pick the song and your tap input. The input list puts your session's mic tracks first, then every audio input on the machine, then Keyboard [SPACE / T] or On-Screen Tap Pad (No Mic Needed). Strike Mic / Drum to Auto-Detect listens to every input and selects whichever one you hit. Set how many countdown clicks you want before the song starts (4 by default, at the session tempo) and how many bars of count-in the finished click should have (0, 1, 2 or 4 bars; 1 by default).
  2. Pick the sound. Choose a preset or any two of the 16 sounds, and where the second sound goes (see the library below). Preview Sound is meant to play two bars of your choice in your headphones.
  3. Tap along. Start Countdown & Tap Along counts you in and plays the song. Play on your chosen drum or mic, or tap SPACE, T or the on-screen pad, with SHIFT+SPACE on transition beats. The window shows taps recorded, your live tempo, the input level and how much of the song you have covered. The more of the song you tap through, the better the click.
  4. Finish & Build Click Track with Gemini analyses everything and drops a new click track directly under the song. The job shows up in the bottom JOBS bar, and one Ctrl+Z undoes it.

The click track library

The sounds are generated in code (human-click-track.js, METRONOME_INSTRUMENTS and synthesizeMetronomeInstrumentHit), not loaded from sample files. Each one starts at full attack on its very first sample, so a click lands exactly on its beat. The 16 sounds come in five groups:

  • Hi-hats and cymbals: closed hi-hat, open hi-hat, pedal hi-hat, ride (bow), ride bell
  • Sticks and shells: drumsticks (stick on stick), snare cross-stick, tight snare, tight kick
  • Bells and blocks: high cowbell, low cowbell, high woodblock, low woodblock
  • Percussion: tambourine, shaker
  • Studio clicks: a classic electronic metronome click

The default pair is a closed hi-hat on ordinary beats and an open hi-hat on the transition beat. Like a real hi-hat, the open hat's ring is cut off ("choked") the moment the next closed hat hits. The test suite checks that the open hat is almost silent (below 0.05) just before beat 1. The other seven presets are ride bow plus ride bell, drumsticks plus open hat, closed hat plus high cowbell, cross-stick plus open hat, low plus high woodblock, the classic click plus high woodblock, and Gemini Smart Pick, which lets Gemini choose the pair that will cut through the song best.

Where the transition sound goes is up to you:

  • Bar turnaround (default): the last beat of every bar, and the beat before a new section, so you hear the bar coming round to beat 1.
  • Downbeat: beat 1 of every bar and the first beat of each new section.
  • Section: only the turnaround at the end of every 4-bar phrase and at section changes.

Run from a terminal, the preview renderer shows the default pattern. The * marks the transition beat, which is the open hat on beat 4 of each bar:

$ node -e "const H=require('./packages/daw-studio/lib/human-click-track'); ..." 16 instruments, 8 presets hihat-closed + hihat-open 4.20s hihat-closed hihat-closed hihat-closed hihat-open* hihat-closed hihat-closed hihat-closed hihat-open*

How your taps and the song are combined

Nobody taps perfectly. You will rush a little, drop a beat, or bounce a stick. fuseHumanTapsWithMusic is written for that. In order, it:

  1. Finds your taps in the recorded input in 3 ms windows (extractHumanTapsFromWav), ignoring hits closer than 140 ms together, and notes how hard each tap was compared to the others. Keyboard and pad taps are added in too.
  2. Works out the tempo you felt from the gaps between taps. A gap about two, three or four times the usual one counts as skipped beats and is divided down, and stray double bounces are thrown away.
  3. Analyses the song with the meter-first beat-grid analyser from the previous release (analyzeAudioBeatGrid in click-track-generator.js), giving it your tempo as a starting hint.
  4. Measures how early or late you are. It slides all your taps up to 70 ms either way against the song's drum hits and keeps the shift that lines up best. Most players are consistently a little ahead or behind, and that constant offset is removed.
  5. Settles half time versus double time by picking the song's strongest tempo candidate within 7% of the tempo you tapped, using a candidate an octave up or down if needed. You decide the feel; the audio supplies the exact number.
  6. Watches for tempo changes with a sliding window over your taps, so a song that speeds up in the chorus gets several tempo sections instead of one average tempo.
  7. Finds beat 1: if you hit harder every 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7 beats, that counts as a vote for the time signature and for where the bar starts. That vote is combined with the downbeat the song analysis found.

Coverage counts too. Taps that span at least 70% of the song are rated full, at least 25% partial, and anything less is just a seed for the audio analysis. The unit test proves the point: tapping the whole song raises coverage by more than 40 points over a short burst of taps.

Where Gemini comes in

Once the numbers are in, generateHumanGuidedClickTrackForProject writes two files to a temporary work folder under ~/.local/share/robos/daw-studio/human-click-analysis/. One is human-tap-fusion.json, holding your tap summary (count, coverage, felt tempo, timing spread, early/late offset, accent hints, local tempo windows), the combined estimate, the audio analysis's top tempo and meter candidates, and the list of available sounds. The other is song-excerpt.wav, about 18 seconds of the song as 16 kHz mono. It then asks the AI provider you picked to read the JSON and reply with only a small JSON object: tempo, time signature, any tempo sections, the two sounds, the transition placement and one sentence of reasoning. The provider list puts Gemini CLI / Antigravity first and falls back to any other provider installed on the machine. A reply is used only if it parses and the tempo is between 42 and 250 BPM (or it returns sections). If no provider answers, the click is built from the combined estimate alone. Either way the work folder is deleted afterwards.

The result is a new track named like Click (124 BPM · 4/4 · Closed Hi-Hat + Open Hi-Hat), placed directly under the song. If you asked for a count-in that is longer than the silence before the song, every clip in the session moves right just far enough to fit it. Songs with tempo changes also get Tempo … BPM markers on the timeline. With Replace any previous generated Click Track ticked (the default), earlier click tracks are removed first.

Camera viewers: double-tap or press the corner icon for fullscreen

The studio uses an Android phone running RobOS IP Camera as the overhead drum camera, and you check the framing from wherever you are: the phone itself, a browser, or the Smart Home app. Commit a80146c0 gives all of those viewers the same control as a video player: a four-corner fullscreen icon in the bottom-right corner of the picture, and double-tap (or double-click) to go fullscreen and back.

  • Camera Watch browser viewer (web-viewer.js): the icon uses the browser's Fullscreen API, falls back to the WebKit version, and if neither works, falls back to a CSS class that pins the picture to the whole window. Double-click works on a desktop. On a phone, two taps within 320 ms count as a double-tap. The icon follows the real fullscreen state, so pressing Esc swaps it back too. We drove this in Electron: a double-click entered fullscreen and swapped the icon, and clicking the icon left fullscreen.
  • RobOS IP Camera's own browser page (HttpOnvifServer.kt) gets the same icon and double-tap.
  • RobOS IP Camera app (Components.kt, Screens.kt, MainActivity.kt): the text "Fullscreen" button on the live preview is now the icon, and double-tapping the preview opens fullscreen. In fullscreen, double-tap now leaves fullscreen (it used to switch between cropped and fitted), and a matching exit icon sits in the corner. One behaviour from the last post is gone: turning the phone sideways no longer jumps to fullscreen by itself. Fullscreen now only happens when you ask for it.
  • Smart Home for Android (CamerasScreen.kt): each camera tile has the icon and responds to double-tap. Both open a full-screen black view of the camera (it used to open a small pop-up card) that refreshes about five times a second, with the camera's name and battery level in a corner. Double-tap or the exit icon closes it.

We captured the browser viewer only. The two Android apps were not built or run for this post.
